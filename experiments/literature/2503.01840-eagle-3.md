# EAGLE-3: Scaling up Inference Acceleration via Training-Time Test (arXiv:2503.01840)

**Year / venue:** 2025, NeurIPS 2025.
**Headline claim:** Up to 6.5x speedup, 1.4x over EAGLE-2 at batch=1, 1.38x throughput improvement in SGLang at batch=64.
**Mechanism:** Two changes from EAGLE-2: (1) abandons the *feature prediction* objective in favor of direct token prediction during draft training; (2) replaces the use of only the second-top-layer feature with a *fusion of low-, mid-, and high-level features* (a learned gate over multiple layers' hidden states). The fusion is enabled by a "training-time test" trick that simulates inference-time error accumulation while training, so the draft sees realistic distributional drift. This unlocks better data scaling: EAGLE saturates at ~tens of millions of tokens, EAGLE-3 keeps improving past 100M+.

**Intel applicability:** HIGH. Same architectural footprint as EAGLE-1/2; only the draft training recipe and the multi-layer feature tap change. The fusion requires the worker to expose 3+ intermediate layer activations to the draft head — a standard hook in OpenVINO IR if you bake it into the export.

**Implementation cost:** MEDIUM. Beyond EAGLE-1/2 work: modify the OV IR export to expose intermediate hidden states from layers ~L/4, ~L/2, ~3L/4 to the draft head input. Re-train heads with the larger dataset and training-time-test loss.

**Open hypothesis it generates for us:** EAGLE-3 reports the gain is largest at large batch sizes because the verifier amortizes better. On charlie running batch=8 (a realistic single-user multi-stream serving load), we expect EAGLE-3 to give ~1.3x over EAGLE-2 and ~5x over baseline. Worth testing whether the multi-layer feature tap on OV IR introduces enough export overhead to cancel the gain (intermediate-tensor outputs sometimes block fusion passes in OV).
