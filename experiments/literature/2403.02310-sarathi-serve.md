# Sarathi-Serve: Throughput-Latency Tradeoff with Chunked Prefill (arXiv:2403.02310)

**Year / venue:** 2024, OSDI 2024.
**Headline claim:** 2.6x serving capacity (Mistral-7B on A100), 3.7x (Yi-34B on dual A100), 5.6x (Falcon-180B with PP). Eliminates prefill stalls.
**Mechanism:** Chunked prefill + stall-free batching. Standard "dynamic batching" stalls all in-flight decodes whenever a new request's prefill arrives (because the prefill is huge and serial). Sarathi-Serve splits each prefill into fixed-size chunks (e.g. 512 tokens) and *piggybacks* one chunk per step into a batch that primarily consists of decode steps. The decode requests get GPU compute that would otherwise sit idle (decode is bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound), and the prefill makes steady progress without stalling anyone. Achieves 95%+ MFU continuously.

**Intel applicability:** HIGH. The technique is purely a *scheduling change* in the serving layer — no kernel work. Especially important for Intel iGPU where the "decode is bandwidth-bound, prefill is compute-bound" asymmetry is even more pronounced than on NVIDIA (Lunar Lake has only ~32-48 GB/s effective memory bandwidth for the iGPU partition, but decent compute via XMX-equivalents). Compute is sitting idle during pure decode batches.

**Implementation cost:** MEDIUM. Tahoma's master/scheduler needs to be rewritten as a continuous-batching loop that mixes prefill chunks with decode steps. The worker needs to handle variable-shape batches (mix of one chunked prefill + N decodes). With OpenVINO's PagedAttention this is mostly a scheduler change.

**Open hypothesis it generates for us:** On alpha at decode-only batch=4, GPU compute utilization is probably ~5-10% (very low). Adding one prefill chunk per step should push utilization to ~80% with no impact on per-decode latency. Hypothesis: chunked prefill on alpha gives 1.5-2x better requests/sec at the same per-token TPOT, even with a small fleet. Worth treating as the *default* scheduler design from day one rather than retrofitting.
