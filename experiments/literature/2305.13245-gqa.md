# GQA: Grouped-Query Attention (arXiv:2305.13245)

**Year / venue:** 2023, EMNLP 2023.
**Headline claim:** Quality close to MHA at the speed of MQA. Uptraining cost only 5% of original pre-training compute.
**Mechanism:** Architectural change in attention: split Q heads into G groups, share one K/V head per group. Reduces KV-cache size from `n_q_heads × head_dim` per token to `G × head_dim` per token (typically 4-8x reduction). Crucially, keeps quality near MHA (unlike MQA which can lose meaningful accuracy). Now baked into Llama-2/3, Mistral, Qwen, etc. — most modern open models ship with GQA already.

**Intel applicability:** N/A as a research bet; GQA is *already in the model checkpoints we serve*. But it's load-bearing for understanding why decode is bandwidth-bound on Intel iGPU: GQA already cut KV-cache traffic by 4-8x, so further memory-bandwidth optimizations have a diminishing-returns ceiling that GQA partially set. Knowing this helps calibrate expectations for KV-cache quantization / MLA-style compression on top.

**Implementation cost:** TRIVIAL — comes for free with the model. Tahoma's loader just needs to handle the `num_key_value_heads` field in HF configs.

**Open hypothesis it generates for us:** Not a hypothesis, but a *baseline calibration*: when the tahoma docs say "alpha can serve Llama-3-8B at X tok/s," that X already reflects GQA-induced KV traffic reduction. Any new KV-compression technique we evaluate must be benchmarked relative to a GQA model, not a hypothetical MHA model.
