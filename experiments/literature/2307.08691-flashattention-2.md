# FlashAttention-2: Faster Attention with Better Parallelism and Work Partitioning (arXiv:2307.08691)

**Year / venue:** 2023.
**Headline claim:** ~2x over FlashAttention-1, reaching 50-73% of theoretical peak FLOPS on A100. End-to-end LM training at 225 TFLOPS/s/A100 (72% MFU).
**Mechanism:** Three changes from FA-1: (1) reduce *non-matmul* FLOPs (rescaling, normalization) — particularly the rescaling that happens between blocks of the online-softmax. (2) Parallelize across the *sequence* dimension as well as the batch+head dimension, so even a single-head attention can saturate a GPU at long sequences (FA-1 only parallelized over batch and heads). (3) Better *intra-warp* work partitioning: distribute the attention computation across warps so they cooperate via registers rather than shared memory.

**Intel applicability:** MEDIUM. The algorithmic core (online softmax with block-wise SRAM tiling) is portable to any GPU with on-chip memory and fast matmul. OpenVINO already ships its own flash-attention-style kernel for Intel GPUs, called `SDPA` with internal tiling. The *specific* warp partitioning is CUDA-jargon, but the equivalent on Intel is sub-group / EU partitioning, which the OV team has implemented. The takeaway for us: validate that OV's SDPA hits >50% of peak on the target Intel device, which is the FA-2 bar.

**Implementation cost:** TRIVIAL — already there in OpenVINO 2024.x. Our work is benchmarking and ensuring SDPA isn't fragmented by the IR export.

**Open hypothesis it generates for us:** Measure: what fraction of theoretical FLOPS does OV's SDPA achieve on charlie (Battlemage) vs A100? If it's <40% (FA-1 level), there's a kernel optimization opportunity worth filing as an upstream OV issue. If it's >60% (FA-2 level), we don't need to do anything. This is a sanity-check experiment, not a research bet.
